Version 0.1 • 28 October 2026 • Proposed consolidated rules

This rulebook turns the supplied El Dorado Rules draft and the decisions made in our discussion into one playable specification. It covers a full six-faction campaign, a smaller test scenario, and tactical combat. Every numerical value is a first-playtest value, not a claim of balance. The rules are ready for implementation and adjudicated playtesting; they have not been tested with human players.

The six factions, irregular territories, separate influence and political control, religious institutions, faction differences, and tactical battles remain central. Where the original offers alternatives, this version selects one. The original remains the historical design reference; this document is the proposed operative version.

Your decisions take priority: maintenance happens first; all unsupplied forces suffer attrition; a supply route must pass through your influence, political control, or military control; reaching the manpower limit prevents further recruitment; exceeding it after losing territory causes an economic penalty, not automatic disbanding; unused manpower gives no bonus.
Read sections 2–4 for the map and setup, then follow phases 1–6 in sections 5–10. Section 11 is the battle procedure. Sections 12–15 cover fleets, factions, victory, and a complete test map. Sections 16–18 provide worked cases, a disposition of every substantial original subsystem, and source notes.

This is an original adaptation. It borrows strategic ideas from Axis & Allies, Rise and Decline of the Third Reich, and The Napoleonic Wars, and tactical ideas from DBA. It does not require any of those games to play. Its grid, troop values, economy, and integration rules are El Dorado proposals, not official rules of those games.

Rule precedence is: explicit exception in a faction rule, then the specific procedure, then the general rule. A modifier applies once unless expressly stated otherwise. Round fractions up only where stated; otherwise round down. A d6 is a six-sided die. All information is public except unrevealed battle deployment. All random draws use d6s; ties in opposed rolls favor the defender unless a rule says otherwise.
A campaign round represents one season. Four rounds make a year: spring, summer, autumn, winter. A full game lasts 12 rounds. The calendar is a game abstraction and does not claim to reproduce historical dates. Six phases occur each round for all players before the next phase begins.

Roll for the initial first player; ties reroll. Thereafter the first-player marker moves one seat clockwise each round. Use this order for phases 1, 2, 5, and 6. In phases 3 and 4, players alternate one action at a time in that same order until everyone passes. Passing ends your participation in that phase. Unspent actions disappear.

Each faction records gold, manpower capacity, committed manpower, available influence cubes, stability, devotion, prisoners, and a capital or administrative seat. Each army records its units, general marker, traditions, movement already used, and whether it has acted or fought this round. Defending does not consume an activation, but any retreat makes the surviving units spent for the rest of the round; a victorious defender may still activate if it has not already done so. Each fleet records ships, assigned cargo, and movement.

A province records a unique ID, adjacent provinces, terrain, culture, printed population, printed tax, political owner and status, influence by faction, buildings, plague, and occupying armies. A sea zone records its adjacent zones and coastal provinces. Adjacency is authoritative: touching only at a corner does not connect territories.
Influence is local support, represented by cubes. Multiple factions can have influence in one province. Political control has exactly one owner, with either client or annexed status. Home provinces count as annexed. Military control means your army occupies the field after hostile field forces have been defeated or have withdrawn. Military control alone gives neither taxes nor manpower.

A city under siege is a separate location inside its province. The besieger controls the field; the defender controls the city. They may coexist only under the siege rules. Otherwise hostile forces never coexist after an action resolves. Neutral militia are hostile only to a faction currently attacking them. A foreign army with an access agreement is neither a military controller nor an additional supply permission.

An independent province has no political owner. A client remains politically associated with its patron but gives reduced benefits. An annexed province can host recruitment and full administration. Diplomatic influence never by itself removes an opponent's annexed ownership; conquest or rebellion does that.
Printed population is 3, 5, or 7. Plague loss is either 0 or 1. Effective population E is printed population minus plague loss, with a minimum of 1. Total influence in a province cannot exceed E.

If capacity falls below the existing cube count, remove excess cubes one at a time from the faction with the most cubes; ties use player order. A functioning religious institution protects its owner's last cube. If several protected cubes would exceed capacity, only the institution belonging to the political owner remains protected; otherwise the oldest institution wins, with player order resolving a tie. The test scenario permits only one religious institution per province.

Influence cubes removed by conflict, plague, revolt, or capture return to the general supply, not the owner's available pool. Players cannot voluntarily withdraw or trade placed cubes. The explicit relocation action is the sole voluntary exception.
An annexed province contributes E manpower if no enemy occupies its field. A client contributes 1 manpower if it has at least one patron influence cube and no hostile army. Supply is required to recruit locally, but a disconnected province still contributes national manpower. This keeps logistics and national army size separate.

An annexed province pays printed tax multiplied by your influence divided by E, rounded down. A client pays 1 gold if it has at least one patron cube. No province pays you taxes while its field is occupied by an enemy, while its city is besieged, or while you have zero influence there. A functioning city adds 2 gold only when its province otherwise pays you tax. Your held administrative seat adds 8 gold to national income. These values deliberately make full influence economically useful without changing manpower every time one cube moves.

Committed manpower U counts every owned land unit and ship, including embarked troops and purchased units awaiting deployment. Infantry and artillery cost 1 manpower each, cavalry 2, and ships or canoe flotillas 1. Buildings, generals, and prisoners cost none. National capacity M is the sum of eligible provincial contributions. No faction may purchase a unit if the resulting U would exceed M.
The standard scenario uses six factions: Gulf conquistador, Panama conquistador, Triple Alliance, Maya confederation, Inca ruler, and Inca pretender. Six humans may control one each. With five, one player controls both Inca factions as separate rivals, with independent treasuries and no transfers between them; use the two-faction scenario for a clean competitive first test. Shared control of opposing factions is a teaching convenience, not a balanced tournament format.

Each faction begins with two annexed provinces: a population-7 seat with a city and a population-3 supporting province. Each has full influence in both, 60 gold, 4 available influence, stability 2, no devotion, no traditions, and no prisoners. Native seats have a temple; Spanish seats have a port. Place no additional infrastructure unless the map says so.
Each army receives a general marker attached to one of its core-faction infantry units. It is not an extra unit. Native armies begin with 8 manpower committed; conquistadors begin with 9 including ships. Each faction begins with capacity 10. The two Spanish expedition supporting provinces begin with plague stage 1, but do not lose population until the first phase 6 progression.

All player factions begin at war except the two conquistadors, who are permanently barred from attacking each other or each other's seats. They still compete through influence and victory scoring. Independent provinces begin with no influence and no standing army. Their defenders appear when attacked.
Before movement, attrition, recruitment, or new income, calculate upkeep on all deployed units. Infantry and artillery cost 2 gold each; cavalry, ships, and canoe flotillas cost 3. Generals and buildings have no upkeep. Cargo pays its own upkeep in addition to its carrier's upkeep.

Calculate excess X as the larger of 0 and U minus M. The over-limit surcharge is basic upkeep multiplied by X divided by the larger of 1 and M, rounded up. Total maintenance is basic upkeep plus that surcharge. Snapshot U and M before casualties or voluntary disbanding this phase. There is no bonus when U is below M. Newly lost land affects the next maintenance assessment; recruitment restrictions change immediately.

Pay as much as possible. Record unpaid maintenance as arrears, reduce stability by 1, and block all optional gold spending until arrears are cleared. Phase 6 income automatically pays arrears before becoming spendable gold. Existing troops are not removed merely for exceeding manpower. If arrears remain at the following phase 1, after paying the new bill remove one non-artillery land unit from each army as desertion, owner chooses. If an army has only artillery, remove one artillery. These losses do not cancel arrears. Fleets instead lose one ship per fleet. This is a bankruptcy consequence, separate from over-limit status.

Voluntary disbanding is allowed after payment, without refunds. It changes later recruitment and next round's bill, not the bill just assessed. Remove an empty army marker. Apply core-unit cohesion after all phase-1 losses.
A land army is supplied if it can trace a connected route from its province to one of its functioning cities or its administrative seat. A functioning source must be politically yours, have at least one of your influence cubes, and be free of siege and hostile field forces. An isolated city can feed local armies; it does not also have to connect to the capital.

Every land province on the route, including the origin, must contain at least one of your influence cubes, be politically yours, or be militarily controlled by your troops. These are alternatives, not cumulative requirements. Rival influence or political ownership alone does not block a route through your influence. A hostile field army does block it. An enemy-held walled city blocks transit through its province even when besieged; an army besieging that city may trace out of its own province but cannot serve as a transit link for armies beyond it.

Treaties do not automatically lend influence, territory, or supply. You must establish your own qualifying presence. Sea supply connects two coastal endpoints through a continuous chain of sea zones, each containing at least one of your ships, with no hostile fleet. One endpoint must be your functioning port; the other may be a coastal province qualifying through your influence, political ownership, or military occupation. The land route beyond either endpoint follows normal rules. Ships can transport troops and provide supply simultaneously; supply has no per-unit capacity limit.

A fleet is supplied if its own zone is adjacent to your functioning port or connects to one through this same ship chain. Embarked armies use the fleet's supply status. Canoes supply only adjacent coastal endpoints and cannot extend a multi-zone supply chain.
Every unsupplied army loses one unit for each four land units it contains, rounded up, owner chooses. Every unsupplied fleet loses one ship for each four ships, rounded up. Reassign cargo among surviving ships in that fleet; excess cargo is lost. Embarked armies also suffer their own unsupplied attrition; the carrier is not a substitute for feeding its passengers.

All losses are assessed from the pre-attrition snapshot and then applied. Therefore destroying a supply ship during this step changes next round's attrition assessment, not other losses already calculated. Recalculate supply before each battle and before recruitment or construction placement. Newly cut supply immediately affects combat and placement, but attrition happens only in phase 1. In battle, an unsupplied force has −1 on every combat total and −1 tactical command point, minimum 1.
Each purchase names a destination province and a unit type or project. Pay now; there are no credit purchases. Reserve the unit's manpower immediately. All destinations must be annexed and held since the start of this round, supplied, and free of hostile forces or siege. A religious project in a client province is the single construction exception.

Each province may deploy new units with total manpower no greater than E per round. Ships use the same local quota as land troops. A port may also deploy no more than two new ships per round. Purchased units appear in phase 5, and completed structures in phase 6. They cannot act in the round of purchase. A newly completed city or port cannot host a purchase already ordered that round.
One city, one wall, one port, and one religious institution may exist per province. One road may exist per land border. At most one project may be purchased in a province per round; a road uses the quota of both endpoints. A mission requires Spanish ownership and a temple native ownership. Founding either requires one of your armies to remain there through completion; upgrading ordinary infrastructure does not.

Native factions recruit their own culture's spear, bow, and skirmisher profiles; Inca may also recruit slingers, and the Triple Alliance its two elites. Owning a client or annexed province of another native culture unlocks its ordinary profiles as allied units, not its faction bonuses. Spanish core units and ships are recruited only at the Spanish faction's seat or another annexed port city. Native allies may be recruited in supplied annexed provinces of their culture. No one recruits units in clients. Religious institutions and culture access do not change a unit’s core nationality: native allies always remain allied units when hired by a Spanish player.

All armies must contain at least one core-faction unit. The two Inca factions count Inca units as core. Culture access is checked at purchase and again at deployment. At the end of any action or phase in which an army loses its last core unit, all remaining allied units desert. A surviving core unit may be transferred from a co-located friendly army before applying this check.

In phase 2 you may also buy up to three available influence cubes for 3 gold each. Generated influence is not subject to this purchase limit. The available influence pool is capped at 12; excess generation is lost. This cap prevents indefinite hoarding.
Every faction receives four influence action points. Spend one devotion token for two additional points, once per phase. No die roll, weather, or army-size modifier changes the strategic action budget. Players alternate actions and pay their full point cost immediately.

A target province is reachable if you already have influence there, own it politically, occupy it militarily, or it is adjacent to a province where you have influence. Overseas placement requires your supply-eligible ship chain between coastal endpoints. Rival armies bar peaceful placement unless they grant access under a treaty; they do not bar a contest in a province where you already have influence. Influence placement or contesting must remain connected to your administrative seat, a functioning institution of yours, or your army through provinces containing your own influence or political control. A local army can therefore establish a new bridgehead of influence. Relocation cannot remove the last cube that qualifies the target as reachable. No influence can be added while a hostile walled city remains in the province.
A contest costs no cube from the available pool. For a contest, each side rolls d6. Add +1 for your own army in the province and +1 if you are its political owner. No other strategic combat modifiers apply. The attacker removes one defender cube only if its total is strictly higher. Ties leave everything unchanged. Removing a protected last cube is illegal. A successful contest does not automatically place your cube; that requires another action and an available cube.

Maya influence costs one point in jungle. Other factions pay two points to place or contest influence in jungle. Relocation into jungle by a non-Maya costs three. Actions never remove a protected cube; destroy the hostile institution militarily first.
An independent province becomes your client automatically when you have at least a majority of its influence capacity, have more cubes than every rival, and no hostile army or hostile religious institution is present. Majority means strictly more than half E. Pay the client-agreement action; no additional die roll is needed.

A client may switch patrons if another player reaches those conditions and pays the action. The old patron loses political control immediately. An annexed province cannot be switched this way.

Annexation requires your cubes to fill all E influence slots, no rival institution, no siege, and either your army in the province or an existing client relationship that began before this round. Pay two influence points and 4 gold; success is automatic. This replaces several conflicting threshold and die-roll rules in the original. Home provinces do not require re-annexation unless their ownership was lost.

During military conquest, the defeated owner's political marker is removed, but the victor must integrate the province through this procedure. After phase 3 ends, each province occupied by one of your armies gains one free influence cube for you, provided it contains no hostile walled city. If full, remove one unprotected rival cube first, owner chooses; if all cubes are protected, nothing happens. This is once per province, not once per army. No extra annexation actions occur after this free placement.
Two players may announce a truce during phase 3. It takes effect immediately and lasts through the end of the next round unless both extend it. A truce forbids attacks, military suppression, blockades, and destruction against the partner. Ending hostilities lifts existing sieges immediately: the former besieger withdraws to a legal adjacent province, and a fleet must withdraw from any zone where it solely blockades the partner. If no legal withdrawal exists, the truce cannot be made. Fleets of different owners may share a zone under a truce without fighting or blocking one another; they never combine supply ships or combat strength. Influence contests remain legal. No automatic allied movement, combined armies, or shared supply results.

A separate access agreement permits peaceful movement through the partner's provinces. Armies may pass through but must end in their own province or an independent province; they may not end with the partner's army. If there is no legal end location, the move is illegal. No combined attacks or defensive coalitions are used in version 0.1.

A player may break a truce before its own military action, losing 2 stability. The permanent Spanish prohibition cannot be broken. The declaration applies before moving and permits the target to act normally afterward. At phase 3, each pair may transfer gold once by mutual agreement. Influence, units, prisoners, territory, and future actions cannot be transferred. Promises about future play are unenforceable; immediate gold transfers are final.
Each faction has four military command points, plus two if it spends one devotion token. Alternate one action at a time. One command point activates one army or fleet once for this phase. An army may instead fortify, suppress influence, destroy a structure, prepare a siege, or assault a besieged city. Fighting ends the activation. Units that have acted cannot be refreshed by merging, splitting, embarking, or changing army markers.

An army contains at most 10 land units. A province may hold at most 20 of one faction's land units, grouped into at most two armies. All armies in a province defend together. Creating or removing an army marker and redistributing co-located units is free at the start of your activation, but every unit retains its own action status and movement already spent. The activated set contains at most 10 units. New generals attach freely to a core unit in each new army; no additional combat units appear.

Ordinary armies have two campaign movement points; all-cavalry armies have three; any army with cannon has one. Entering open terrain costs one, and forest, jungle, desert, or mountain costs two. Core Inca units treat mountains as open, core Maya jungle/forest as open, and core Triple Alliance desert as open; an army gets the benefit only if every unit qualifies. A road makes its border crossing cost one regardless of terrain. A cannon army may always cross one land border per activation, ending there, even into terrain costing two. This exception avoids immobile artillery.

Entry into an independent or hostile province ends movement and resolves its defenders immediately. Entering an empty enemy province is still a conquest and ends movement. Entering an already occupied independent province of your own does not stop movement. There are no simultaneous orders, interception reactions, or battles between crossing armies: complete each action before the next begins.

Armies on access agreements cannot attack from a partner's province unless they declare the truce broken before the activation. Independent territory cannot be crossed peacefully without first becoming your client or being occupied. Movement through your own clients is allowed.
An independent province attacked for the first time in a round raises E ordinary native units, capped at 7: half spear rounded up and the rest bow, or slingers in Inca culture. These are temporary militia with no upkeep or manpower cost. They are treated as supplied while defending their own independent province; besieged independent militia follow the siege supply rules instead. The next player after the attacker controls them; in two-player play the opponent does. They defend at normal strength with one general marker. A province cannot raise a fresh army again in the same round. Surviving militia that win return to their province; they do not retreat into another faction’s land. Militia that lose disperse and are removed rather than using campaign retreat. Remove surviving militia when it is no longer under attack or siege; casualties do not permanently reduce population.

On conquest, remove the defeated owner's political marker, remove half that owner's influence cubes rounded down, and add one of the victor's cubes from general supply. If no slot exists, replace one unprotected defeated-owner cube; if that is impossible, omit the new cube. Third-party cubes remain. Buildings survive unless expressly destroyed. A captured hostile temple or mission keeps its original religious affiliation and protected cube, but produces nothing while outside that owner's political control.

Occupation provides supply-route qualification but no taxes or manpower. Independent occupied territory does not spawn endless automatic militia; resistance is handled by unrest. If the occupier leaves before political integration, the province stays independent with its existing influence.
Fortify: remain in place and gain an entrenchment marker. In the next battle defending that province, two of your units of your choice receive +1 combat while in their original deployment cells. The marker disappears when the army moves or after that battle. It cannot stack with walls or another entrenchment.

Suppress: an army removes one unprotected rival influence cube from its province automatically. This is a hostile act against that faction. It does not add your influence.

Destroy: remove one mission, temple, city, port, wall, or road endpoint in your province. You must control its field, and no hostile walled city may remain. Destroying a city also destroys its walls and port. Destroying a religious institution removes its protected status but does not automatically remove its cube. Gain 4 gold for razing a city or port; other destruction gives no gold. You may not destroy your own buildings for loot. Roads require both endpoint markers; losing either removes that road.
Before a field battle, defenders in a walled city may withdraw up to six land units into it. Cavalry count as one place, but fight on foot at their normal foot-opponent factor. Remaining troops fight outside. The attacker may occupy the field without fighting those inside. Political ownership remains with the city holder until the city falls, although hostile field occupation suspends tax and manpower contributions. The city holder loses no influence from field battles or failed assaults during this siege; apply one halving when the city finally falls. Siege status starts automatically if at least one hostile land unit remains outside.

The defender's only supply is an unblocked port in the city connected by its own naval supply chain to another supplied port. The city cannot supply itself while besieged. Besieger supply follows the normal origin exception in section 5.2. Both sides pay upkeep; every unsupplied force suffers phase-1 attrition.

A cannon army can spend an activation to assault immediately. An army without cannon must first spend an activation preparing the siege; on a later round it may assault. Preparation is lost if the siege is lifted. Resolve an assault on the standard battle grid. Defenders receive +1 combat while in their deployment cells. One attacking cannon cancels this bonus for one nominated defending unit per active half-round; nominate before any attacks. No separate breach geometry is used.

The besieger cannot add influence or complete buildings in the province until the city falls. City defenders may spend their activation to sortie against the besieger; if they lose and the city still has space, surviving units retreat inside. Relief armies fight the besieger in the field, with the city garrison remaining outside that battle. If the besieger loses, the siege ends. If the garrison surrenders or is eliminated, resolve conquest of the city and province; apply influence losses only once for the whole conquest, not once per field and city battle.
Recalculate M and U, including plague already present and current political ownership. Purchased manpower was reserved, but eligibility must still hold. Deploy in purchase order chosen during phase 2. Each purchase must fit the current national capacity, its named province's local quota, and the province's political, supply, cultural, and building requirements. The province must have remained yours continuously since the round began.

To test a queued unit, compare manpower deployed at the start of this phase plus all earlier successfully deployed purchases plus that unit with M. Later queued units do not block earlier ones. If the test fails, cancel that purchase. Existing units remain even when above M. Canceled purchases and purchases whose destinations were lost are discarded without refund; release their reserved manpower. Purchases may not be moved to another location or carried into the next round.

Add recruits to an eligible local army or create one, with a core unit and the usual capacity limits. If a purchase cannot form or join a legal army, it is canceled. New ships appear in a non-hostile sea zone adjoining their named port, and may form a new fleet. You cannot deploy aboard a transport or directly into a besieged city. Reinforcements cannot move, fight offensively, or use military actions until next round; they defend normally if subsequently attacked by an event, although this edition's event table creates no such immediate attacks.
Complete projects in player order. Recheck the original location and all prerequisites, including continuous ownership and supply. Failed projects are lost without refund. Newly completed buildings function immediately for this phase's income and influence, but become recruitment sites next round. Military units are never required to spend a second activation on a purchased project; religious foundations only require their army to have remained present.

Plague uses four states: untouched, stage 1, stage 2, and recovered. Stage 1 has no population penalty; stage 2 and recovered have a permanent loss of 1 population. Plague loss is capped at 1 per province. Recovered provinces cannot be infected again during this game.

At phase 6, snapshot all stage-1 and stage-2 provinces. Each attempts to infect one adjacent untouched land province: choose the highest effective population, then lowest province ID; infect on 4–6. Multiple successful attempts against one target still place only one stage-1 marker. Newly infected provinces do not spread or advance this round. Existing stage 1 becomes stage 2; existing stage 2 becomes recovered. Then adjust influence capacity and manpower. Plague never removes armies directly; its economic and manpower effects may create over-limit penalties next round. Sea travel does not spread it further in this first ruleset beyond the two initial outbreak sites.
Stability ranges from 0 to 3, starting at 2. Unpaid upkeep and broken treaties lower it as specified. It rises through pacification, festivals, or events. It does not change automatically for ordinary battle victories or defeats. At phase 6, each player at stability 0 or 1 tests exactly one vulnerable province they politically own or militarily occupy.

Choose the province with the lowest ratio of own influence to E; ties choose highest printed tax, then lowest ID. Exclude provinces with all E cubes belonging to the player. Roll d6: revolt on 1–2 at stability 1, or 1–4 at stability 0. An army there gives +1 to the roll. No candidate means no test.

On revolt, remove political control, the player's unprotected influence, and any pending project. A religious institution is destroyed in the revolt, so its formerly protected cube is also removed. If an army is present, it withdraws to an adjacent qualifying retreat province without battle or pursuit; if none exists, it loses half its units rounded up and remains as occupier. A revolted province cannot revolt again that round. Rebel armies and bandit stacks are abstracted into this one procedure.
Calculate income using the post-plague, post-unrest state. Pay arrears first and add the remainder to gold. Gold carries over without limit. Then generate two base influence cubes if you hold a supplied administrative seat, plus two per functioning mission or temple that you own in a province you politically control and can supply. Spanish factions with no functioning mission instead gain one influence per supplied ship, maximum two; this is additional to seat generation. A mission ends that temporary ship-generation benefit.

Battle rewards are already added at the time of victory and are not paid twice. Apply the pool cap of 12 after all generation. Native religious sacrifices and Inca religious spending occur here, as specified in section 13.
Spring has no special effect. Summer battles in jungle or forest have rain on 1–3 instead of 1 on d6. Autumn has no separate tax phase because taxes already occur every round. Winter reduces fleet campaign movement from two to one and prevents roads from reducing mountain entry costs for non-Inca units. Winter causes no additional automatic attrition beyond lack of supply.

At the end of every winter, after income and influence, roll one shared d6 event for the next round. Effects expire at that next round's end. No action-card deck is needed.

Check victory after these steps. Advance the season and first player. No event is rolled after the final round.
The battle model uses limited command points, coordinated groups, opposed die totals, troop roles, and recoil or destruction. Exact grid movement, factors, army sizes, weather, campaign casualties, and victory thresholds below are original simplifications for El Dorado. There is no need to consult DBA during a battle. The original hit-point table, activated columns, and separate random morale/rally tables are replaced by this complete procedure.
Use a 12 by 12 square grid. Columns run A–L, rows 1–12. The attacker enters from row 1, the defender from row 12. Attacker deploys in rows 1–3; defender in rows 10–12. Each cell holds one unit. Each unit faces north, south, east, or west. No stacking or diagonal adjacency is used. All deployed campaign units participate, up to 20 per side. A general marker belongs to one participating core unit; with two armies choose one commanding general and disregard the other's bonus for this battle.

Roll d6 for weather before deployment: 1 means rain; summer forest/jungle changes that to 1–3. Rain lasts the entire battle. It prevents shot and cannon shooting but not their melee defense. Cavalry and crossbows remain usable. Roll for terrain initiative: d6 plus 1 if defending a politically owned province, plus 1 if all your units are native to the province's terrain under their faction rule. Higher total places terrain; ties favor the defender.

Open provinces have no terrain patches. Desert has no patches but reduces cavalry movement to two. Forest/jungle places two 2-by-2 rough patches; mountains place two 2-by-2 hill patches. Place patches entirely in rows 5–8, not overlapping, and leave at least two full north–south columns unobstructed. Number coordinates make placement exact. A river-marked campaign border adds a river across row 7, with bridges at D7 and I7. River cells outside the bridges are impassable. Patches cannot cover the river. These fixed bridges replace terrain-choice arguments.

Both sides select deployments secretly and reveal together. At least two units, or all if fewer than two, must be in the front deployment row: row 3 for attacker and row 10 for defender. All face the opposing baseline. A physical game may use a screen or record coordinates; a digital prototype records simultaneous choices. Attacker takes the first half-round. One attacker half-round plus one defender half-round is one full battle round.
The two melee factors mean fighting foot and fighting mounted units. Cannon, shot, and all ordinary infantry count as foot. Movement is the tactical allowance in cells, not campaign movement. Range is measured by Manhattan distance: horizontal plus vertical distance. A dash means no shooting.
Each unit is intact until destroyed or routed off the board. There are no wounds, hit points, ammunition counters, or individual soldiers. A general marker gives its own unit +1 combat, never to adjacent units. If it is lost, command suffers as below; losing it does not itself eliminate the faction.
At the start of each half-round, the active player may withdraw the whole army under section 11.8. Otherwise roll d6 for tactical command points. Subtract 1 if unsupplied and 1 if the commanding general is absent; minimum one point. A general is absent if its unit is destroyed or routed off the board. Command points expire at the end of this half-round.

Spend one point to move a single unit or a group of up to six edge-connected units sharing the same facing. A group must move by the same displacement and end in the same formation; every unit must have a legal path and enough movement. Use the slowest unit's allowance. No unit may move twice in a half-round. A group cannot include units in enemy contact.

Each orthogonal step costs one movement, rough/hill entry costs two. You may step sideways or backward. End facing any cardinal direction; final facing is included in the order. An in-place change of facing without translation also costs one command point and counts as movement. No passing through occupied cells. Crossing a bridge is ordinary movement. A unit must stop on entering any cell edge-adjacent to an enemy. A unit already in enemy contact may pivot but may not translate unless disengaging.

Disengagement costs two command points: move one cell directly backward relative to current facing into an empty legal cell, retaining facing. It consumes that unit's movement for the half-round. If another enemy remains adjacent, the unit is still engaged. A blocked backward cell prevents disengagement. No free attack is triggered; the opposing player's next half-round gives the response.

After movement, resolve the active side's shooting, then close combat, then check army break. Shooting and combat require no command points. This ensures a low command roll limits manoeuvre rather than preventing an engaged army from defending itself.
Each active ranged unit may shoot once if it is not adjacent to an enemy. It may shoot after moving, except cannon may not both move and shoot. Target one enemy within range and within the shooter's forward half-plane: the target must lie at least one row or column in front of its facing, with lateral offset no greater than forward distance. It may not shoot through other units or rough terrain; use a straight center-to-center line. A target inside rough terrain can be shot at only if no other rough cell is crossed. Hill cells block fire through them, but a unit on the first intervening hill may itself be targeted. Lines exactly on an obstruction's edge count as blocked. No shooting into an existing melee.

Roll d6 plus shooting factor against the target's d6 plus its melee factor against foot. Apply the relevant modifiers below. If the shooter does not exceed the target, nothing happens; the target does not return fire. If the shooter wins but does not reach twice the target's total, the target recoils. If the shooter reaches at least twice the target, destroy it. No shooter's loss can be caused by its own shooting roll. Resolve shots one at a time in active player's chosen order; later targets must still be legal.
An active unit adjacent to an enemy selects one opponent, preferably directly in front; if no front enemy exists it may fight an enemy to its side or rear without turning. Each active unit in contact must fight if it has an adjacent enemy still eligible as a principal. A unit may be the principal fighter in only one close-combat exchange per half-round. A unit already used as a principal or supporter cannot be selected again as a principal. If all adjacent enemies have already participated, skip that active unit; do not roll additional attacks. Other adjacent units may contribute flank pressure rather than start repeated exchanges against a principal that already fought. Resolve chosen exchanges one at a time. Remove casualties and carry out recoils immediately.

Each principal rolls d6 plus its melee factor against the opposing unit's type. Apply modifiers, with a minimum final total of 1. A unit supporting by flank pressure may not also be a principal in that half-round. A principal may receive pressure from at most two supporters. A supporter must have its front edge against the enemy's side or rear edge and must not be adjacent to any other enemy. Each supporter gives the enemy −1. Supporters do not roll or suffer that exchange's outcome.
Side/rear engagement and flank pressure may both apply, maximum combined penalty −2. Units do not gain bonuses simply for being the strategic defender. Spears gain their mounted factor without needing an additional charge-defense rule. Native terrain abilities remove rough/hill movement penalties for the qualifying unit, but do not grant extra combat bonuses except where section 13 expressly says so. The cannon wall-cancellation rule affects only the defensive wall bonus.

If totals tie, neither principal moves. If one total is higher but less than twice the other, the loser recoils. If at least twice, the loser is destroyed. Both sides participate in one opposed exchange; there is no separate counterattack roll. Jaguar warriors that win their own frontal melee against foot instead destroy the enemy on a margin of 3 or more, even if not doubled. This is their single shock ability.
A recoil moves one cell directly away from the opposing principal or shooter. Choose the direction along the larger coordinate difference; equal differences use the target's rear direction if possible, otherwise the direction toward its own baseline. The recoiling unit keeps its facing. If the destination is occupied or impassable, the unit is destroyed. There is no chain pushing or pass-through exception.

Recoil off any board edge routes the unit: put it in that side's routed pool rather than destroying it. Skirmishers and eagle warriors may choose to flee two cells instead of recoiling one, following the same direction. Both cells must be empty and passable; leaving the board routes the unit. If the two-cell route is unavailable, apply ordinary recoil. A unit voluntarily fleeing on the board remains available for later orders.

Destroyed units are removed from the army. Routed units count as losses for battle-break purposes but normally survive into the campaign retreat. A unit destroyed in close combat may become a prisoner under section 13; prisoner status does not restore it to military service.
At battle start record each side's deployed unit count N. Its break threshold is one third N, rounded up. After every half-round, if destroyed plus routed units reach that threshold, that side breaks. If both reach it in the same half-round, both withdraw; no winner, influence rewards, traditions, or pursuit. In a contested independent province both return to their origins; a political defender returns inside its city if possible, otherwise retreats normally. If only one breaks, the other wins.

A voluntary withdrawal at the start of your half-round also loses the battle. After 12 full rounds, if neither side has broken or withdrawn, the defender wins and the attacker withdraws. This hard limit prevents indefinite avoidance.

Survivors, including routed units, retreat together to one adjacent province with no hostile army and with their own influence, political control, or military control. Attacker must use its entry province if legal; defender chooses. If over the destination's 20-unit limit, excess units are destroyed, owner chooses. A besieged garrison may retreat only through its supplied port on available ships or by surrendering; sortie survivors can return inside their city as stated earlier.

An amphibious attacker may retreat to its original ships if they remain and have space. A field defender cannot spontaneously embark without its own adjacent fleet and enough capacity. With no legal retreat, all remaining units are lost; the winner may capture at most two eligible infantry units and the rest are removed. Apply core-unit cohesion after retreat and pursuit.
Only a winner with surviving cavalry may pursue. It rolls one d6 per surviving cavalry, maximum two dice. Each 5–6 destroys one retreating enemy land unit, loser chooses. Pursuit is forbidden in forest, jungle, mountains, rain, city assaults, naval retreats, or mutual withdrawal. It cannot kill more units than remain. This replaces the original unusually severe pursuit table.

The winner receives one available influence cube, or two if Spanish. Except for a political owner still holding a walled city under section 8.4, the loser removes half its cubes in the battle province, rounded down; thus three cubes lose one. If conquest also occurs, apply the halving once, not twice. Winning without conquest still gives the battle reward and halving. A victory against an empty province gives no battle reward or tradition.

After a victory over at least three opposing deployed units, choose one surviving participating army that has fewer than two traditions. Roll d6: 1–2 gives Scouts, 3–4 gives Discipline, 5–6 gives Engineers. Duplicate results give nothing. Scouts allow one unit per battle to move one extra cell during one order; Discipline allows one combat die reroll per battle, keeping the new result; Engineers allow a non-cannon army to assault after preparing in the same round only if it has another command point and has not fought. This is the sole exception to one activation per army: the second activation must be that assault. If an army loses a battle, remove one of its traditions at random. Traditions stay with the army containing its original general-bearing unit when split, and merging retains at most two.

A lost general marker is replaced for free at the next phase 1 on an existing core unit. It cannot be replaced during a battle. If the host unit was captured, the general has no separate ransom value. Rulers are abstract political identities and cannot be killed by a random casualty.
A fleet contains one to four ships or canoe flotillas of the same owner. A sea zone can contain at most eight of one owner's vessels. Splitting and merging follow the army activation rules. Ships move two sea-zone borders per activation, one in winter or storms. Canoes move one and may use coastal zones only. Mark all scenario sea zones as coastal; a future ocean map must mark which are not.

Each ship carries three land units, with cavalry and cannon using two spaces each. Each canoe carries one native infantry unit. Assign cargo to specific carriers when loading. Loading and unloading occur at adjacent coastal provinces and consume the army's entire activation for the round, but may be attached to the fleet's activation without a second command point. The fleet may load, move, and unload once each in that order. A unit may not move by land and embark in the same round. It may not move after unloading. Landing in hostile or independent territory triggers a battle after any naval battle is resolved.

Transport requires control of the departure field and no hostile fleet in the loading zone. Unloading requires a clear or successfully won sea zone. If the fleet fights at sea, its activation ends and it cannot unload until next round. Coastal defenses do not shoot at fleets. A port is not required to land troops, but is required to build ships and provide the source endpoint of naval supply.
Resolve naval encounters with paired opposed rolls using the same intact-or-lost philosophy as land combat. All vessels of each side in the zone participate. Defender pairs one vessel against each attacker until one side has no unpaired vessels. Unpaired vessels give +1 each to a chosen friendly pair, maximum +2 per pair. Each vessel supports or fights once per naval round.

Each pair rolls d6 plus 3 for a ship or plus 1 for a canoe, plus support and minus 1 if unsupplied. Minimum total 1. A tie has no effect; a higher total forces the loser to disengage from that naval round; a doubled total sinks it. Apply all pair outcomes simultaneously, allowing a vessel sunk this round to have resolved its own exchange. Disengaged vessels cannot support later pairs that round, but return if another naval round occurs.

After the round, attacker may retreat, then defender may retreat. A side with no surviving vessels loses immediately. A side must retreat if all surviving vessels disengaged that round. If both must retreat, both leave without a winner. If one side is entirely sunk, the other wins without being forced to retreat; if both are entirely sunk there is no winner. After three naval rounds, a remaining attacker retreats. Re-pair surviving vessels each round. Retreat is one sea zone to a non-hostile zone, attacker returning by its entry route if possible. With no legal zone, survivors are sunk. A winner occupies the zone; no tactical pursuit occurs.

Cargo on sunk vessels is lost. Cargo on surviving vessels retreats with them. Cargo cannot fight at sea and cannon give no extra naval bonus. A naval victory gives the normal battle influence reward, but no land influence removal or army tradition. A port is blockaded whenever an enemy fleet occupies its adjacent sea zone; where a port touches multiple zones, every zone must be hostile to block all routes. Ships in port are represented in its adjoining zone and can be attacked there; there is no protected harbor sub-location.
All faction abilities apply only to that faction's core units unless expressly stated otherwise. Do not stack multiple copies of a bonus. Religion produces influence as a game mechanism; these abstractions are not claims about the peoples represented.
Core troops ignore desert campaign terrain costs. Jaguar and eagle profiles supply their elite advantages. After destroying enemy infantry in close combat, a Triple Alliance or Maya player may immediately roll one d6 to capture it: on 5–6 it becomes one prisoner token; otherwise it remains destroyed. No capture roll is made after shooting, plague, desertion, or naval loss. Maximum two prisoners per battle. Capture does not undo the kill or change battle-break counts.

A prisoner records whether its original unit was Spanish core or native. Prisoners travel with the capturing army, maximum four per army, without using army or transport capacity. If that army is defeated, prisoners are freed and removed. They never become free replacement troops. Unused prisoners may stay with their army indefinitely; there is no separate prison inventory at temples. Transfer prisoners only between your co-located armies, before an activation. A supplied army at your functioning temple may deliver them in phase 6.

Each temple may consume up to two prisoners per round, each yielding one available influence for a native prisoner or two for a Spanish-core prisoner. Triple Alliance must offer at least one prisoner or one own native infantry unit each phase 6. An own unit yields one influence. If no offering is made, remove one unprotected own cube from a politically owned province; choose lowest own influence, then lowest ID. If all cubes are protected, lose one available influence instead, minimum zero. Maya has no compulsory offering.
Core troops ignore forest and jungle campaign and tactical movement penalties. Other factions pay extra influence actions in jungle as already specified. When defending in forest or jungle, Maya may mark one rough cell as a visible trap before deployment. The first enemy entering it stops and cannot shoot during that half-round; remove the trap. It causes no direct casualty. Poison is represented by this disruption and the skirmisher profile; it adds no separate damage track. Maya can build canoes as specified.
Both factions' core troops ignore mountain entry costs and hill tactical movement penalties. Every phase 6, each may pay 4 gold for two additional influence, representing religious patronage. If paid last round but not this round, lose one available influence; no debt or permanent accumulating penalty arises. Keep a single paid-last-round marker.

In summer, each Inca faction may hold its festival for 6 gold. Gain one stability and one devotion token. Declining costs one stability. No other round has a mandatory festival. Payments are optional and prohibited while in arrears. The two factions use identical abilities in this first version; their locations and civil war create the asymmetry. This replaces separate huaca, ancestor, chosen-women, and child-sacrifice accounting.
Spanish armies gain two influence per battle victory and have access to heavy foot, crossbows, shot, cannon, cavalry, and ships. During rounds 1–4, the first successful Spanish shot, cannon shot, or cavalry melee in a land battle forces one extra recoil of the defeated target if it survives and a recoil is legal. Apply the second recoil in the same direction; a blocked second recoil removes the target to its routed pool, not destruction. This represents a temporary shock effect and never applies after round 4.

Each conquistador's starting administrative seat is a Crown base. The two conquistadors cannot attack one another, but can compete for clients and influence in independent provinces. Their two starting seats cannot be politically transferred by influence; another native faction may conquer them normally. This replaces the unresolved contest for a shared Cuba/Panama governorship with one protected home base per Spanish player.
A faction that initiated no hostile military action during phase 4 may pay 4 gold in phase 6 to gain one devotion token, maximum two held. Defending does not disqualify it. Initiating attacks, siege assaults, suppression, destruction, or blockade does. A festival token counts against the same cap. Spending devotion adds two points to one chosen influence or military phase; at most one token per phase. The various moon, Easter, and holiday prohibitions are replaced by this voluntary observance rule, so the calendar cannot accidentally prevent self-defense.
Your administrative seat is a designated city. Losing its political control immediately removes its 8-gold income bonus and seat influence generation. You retain all other provincial income, units, and recruitment. At the next phase 3, you may designate another owned supplied city as seat for two influence actions and 8 gold. This does not create a new victory objective. You cannot relocate while still holding the old seat.

A native faction is eliminated at phase 6 only if it has no politically controlled provinces and no land units. A Spanish faction is eliminated only if it has no politically controlled provinces, no Spanish-core land units, and no ships. Remove its residual cubes, prisoners, and institutions; remaining native allied units dissolve. Its cities, roads, and ports remain in the province as unowned infrastructure. Pending purchases do not postpone elimination. Losing a capital alone is not elimination.

At the end of round 12, score each remaining faction: 1 point per client, 2 per annexed province, plus 3 per original enemy administrative-seat province annexed, plus 1 for every 10 gold remaining, capped at 5 treasury points. Subtract 1 for every 10 gold of arrears, rounded up. No points for mere occupation, influence without political control, or your original seat bonus. Highest score wins; ties favor greater effective population in annexed land, then more remaining gold, then shared victory.

Early victory occurs at phase 6 if one faction has annexed at least 12 of the standard map's 24 provinces, including two original enemy seats, and holds its own original seat. It must satisfy all conditions at two consecutive round ends. Mark the first qualifying round publicly. Loss of any condition clears the marker. Last remaining non-eliminated faction also wins immediately. Truces never combine scores.
This is an abstract geographical scenario for verifying the rules, not a historical reconstruction. It gives every location, population, adjacency, and starting owner, so a first playtest does not depend on a missing map file. Draw the territories as irregular regions, or use labeled nodes until art exists. Only the listed connections count.

Every province's printed tax is twice printed population. Every seat has population 7, each supporting province 3, and every neutral province 3 except N5 and N7, which have 5. Capitals and support provinces are annexed at setup with full owner influence. Other provinces are independent and empty. All provinces are coastal only if a sea-zone listing below says so.
Land borders are undirected. List each once: G2–N1, G2–N3, A1–A2, A2–N1, A2–N2, A1–N4, N1–N2, N1–N3, N2–N4, N3–M1, M1–M2, M2–N5, N3–N5, N4–N5, N5–N6, N6–P1, P1–P2, P2–N7, P2–N8, N6–N7, N7–N8, N7–R1, R1–R2, R2–N9, N8–N9, N9–N10, N10–I1, I1–I2, I2–N11, N10–N11, N10–N12, N12–R2, N11–N12. G1 is an island with no land border; its connection to G2 requires naval transport or a naval supply route. Mark N5–N6 and N9–N10 as river crossings. No roads start on the map.

There are four coastal sea zones: S1 Gulf, S2 Caribbean, S3 Panama Pacific, S4 Southern Pacific. Sea adjacency is S1–S2, S2–S3, S3–S4; S2–S3 is an abstract navigable connection for the prototype, not a historical canal claim. S1 coasts are G1, G2, N2, M1. S2 coasts are M1, N6, P1. S3 coasts are P1, P2, N8, R1. S4 coasts are N8, N11, I2. Gulf ships start in S1 and Panama ships in S3. For its initial two ships the Panama base uses its S3 port frontage. A port touching two zones may use either.
Use Gulf conquistador versus Triple Alliance on G1, G2, A1, A2, N1, N2, N3, N4 and S1. Retain only borders and coasts among these locations. Use their standard forces, gold, influence, institutions, and starting plague. N3 is an ordinary Maya-culture independent province. Play six rounds starting in spring. Use the same phase procedures, tactical combat, and scoring. Early victory requires annexing six of these eight provinces including the enemy seat at two consecutive round ends while holding your own seat. This scenario is intended to expose rules problems before six-player diplomacy is tested.
An army in an occupied independent province traces through a province containing one of its influence cubes, then through its own client, to its own supplied city. This is valid even if the influence-only province has another political owner. An enemy field army appearing in that intermediate province blocks the route. A hostile walled city there also blocks transit. Merely having an access treaty does not create a fourth supply permission.
A faction starts with capacity 12 and 10 committed manpower. It buys two infantry, reserving both remaining points. In phase 4 it loses a province worth 3 capacity: M becomes 9. Its 10 deployed manpower remains. Both queued units fail phase 5 and are discarded. Next phase 1, if basic upkeep is 20, excess is 1 and surcharge is ceiling of 20 × 1 ÷ 9, or 3. Total upkeep is 23. There is no automatic loss of an existing army for exceeding capacity.
A five-unit unsupplied army owes its full upkeep before it loses two units to attrition. The losses free manpower for phase 2 but do not refund maintenance. Reconnecting it during phase 4 removes its unsupplied combat penalty immediately. It receives no replacement for the units already lost.
A population-3 independent province with two of your cubes and one empty slot can become a client for one action. The client yields 1 manpower and 1 gold. Placing your third cube fills the province. With an army present, spend two actions and 4 gold to annex; without an army, wait until a later round after the client agreement. Annexation gives 3 manpower and, at full influence, its full 6 gold tax. A rival can contest its cubes but cannot simply flip its annexed political ownership.
A spear facing cavalry uses factor 5. It rolls 3, totaling 8. The cavalry uses its foot-opponent factor 3 and rolls 2, totaling 5: cavalry recoils because 8 is greater than 5 but less than twice 5. If cavalry instead totals 4, the spear's 8 doubles it and destroys it. If a recoil cell is blocked by a friendly unit, the recoiling unit is destroyed; no undefined pushing sequence follows.
An eight-unit force breaks after three destroyed or routed units. If two were destroyed and one routed off the grid, the routed unit returns with the five on-board survivors during the campaign retreat: six survive before pursuit. A victorious cavalry force may inflict at most two additional pursuit losses. Loss of its final core unit then causes allied-unit desertion under the cohesion rule.
Verify one complete round with no war, a conquest and annexation over two rounds, a supply cut, a siege with naval supply, a failed deployment, a fleet sinking with cargo, a revolt, a plague spread, and a forced retreat with no destination. Check that all spending is affordable, manpower is never double-counted, each unit activates only once, and phase-6 income is not available to phase-2 purchases in the same round.

Track battle length, first-player win rate, Spanish ranged-unit effectiveness, native capture income, the rate of political integration, and how often a lost province produces an unrecoverable bankruptcy spiral. Tune prices, thresholds, and rates after observing play; do not add another subsystem to fix a numerical imbalance. The design is specified, but these balance and enjoyment questions remain empirical.
This disposition covers the substantial mechanics in the supplied document. A replacement is deliberate; absence from the operative procedures is not permission to revive an older alternative mid-game.
These simplifications are proposed design decisions, not additional user-approved constraints. The highest-impact ones to revisit after the first tests are automatic political integration, the loss of independent commodity trading, and the replacement of hit points with recoil/destruction.
